01 · The game, four ways

One game. Four crowds.

Four boxes, one family. Click a box to see its cards and coupons. Each version keeps the seven categories and the scoring, and swaps in cards and coupons for who's playing.

02 · Partner rewards

Win the coupon. Get the real thing.

Still a Chaotic Mischief coupon, with a touch of the partner: their colour on the edge, their name on the ticket, and a code to redeem in real life. The partner pays to be in the deck and sees every redemption.

1

A partner buys a slot

A smoothie bar, studio or app sponsors a coupon in a print run.

2

Players earn it

Every 7 Chaos Credits earns a coupon. Some are real.

3

They show up

Scan the barcode, redeem in store or in the app.

4

Everyone wins

Players get a reward, partners get customers, our cost per deck drops.

03 · Limited drops

Tiny, numbered, local. Free to play.

Each drop is a promo piece for one place or one moment: 25 decks for a single lounge, 100 for one store, 500 for one event, 1,000 for an online sign-up push. Small runs are cheap for the brand and easy to hype, because almost nobody gets one. The game, the rules and the chaos stay Chaotic Mischief; the look, the logo, the cards and the coupons belong to the brand.

To see the real logos: click a drop's logo slot (or drag the file onto it) and add the logo file from that brand's press or media page. It fills the box, cards, coupons and story. You can drop a campaign photo onto the hero too.

04 · Proof it works

Two models we're borrowing from

Supreme shows how limited drops turn a product into an event. Free Bean shows brands will pay for a free product people actually want.

Supreme drops

Scarcity plus collaborations make a moment people line up for.
  • Weekly drops: new items release on a set day in small quantities, and sell out.
  • Collaborations with brands far outside streetwear turned each drop into a story.
  • It built real value: VF Corporation bought Supreme for about $2.1 billion in 2020, and EssilorLuxottica bought it for about $1.5 billion in 2024.

For us: numbered runs, one partner per drop, a reason it exists, gone when it's gone.

Free Bean

05 · The order matters

How we get there

Brands sponsor things that already have fans, so drops come after the game proves itself.

1

Launch the game

Kickstarter, about 2,000 decks. Creators and friends make it the thing to play.

2

Prove it

Clips that travel, an email list, shops reordering.

3

First local partner

One partner coupon with a Kits business. Measure redemptions.

4

First drop

25 to 100 numbered decks at one partner location, free, paid for by the partner.
